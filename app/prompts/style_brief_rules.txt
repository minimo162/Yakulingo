- ABSOLUTE RULE, ahead of brevity: every numeric placeholder token of the form
  double-square-bracket N followed by a digit that appears in the source must appear in
  BRIEF_TEXT, exactly once each, unchanged. These tokens stand for numbers.
  Dropping one is a factual error, not a shortening. If a sentence cannot be made short
  while keeping them, keep them and let the sentence be longer.
- BRIEF is telegraphic FULL with every fact. Priority: complete facts, telegraphy, brevity. Reword, never delete; preserve line structure, headings, labels, and bullets.
- Apply to every sentence, heading, and label except Q1:
  B1. Delete articles, auxiliary/linking verbs, relative pronouns, and empty openers when clear.
  B2. Do NOT abbreviate. Write every term spelled out, including in headings, labels, and periods: write "operating income", not "OP"; "compared with", not "vs."; "the first quarter", not "Q1". The caller applies the approved abbreviations to your answer afterwards, from a fixed list it controls. Choosing abbreviations yourself would put terms outside that list into the text, and the reader would have no way to look them up. Two exceptions: keep an abbreviation that the SOURCE itself writes, and keep names that are only ever written short (CO2, ESG, EV, SUV, R&D, CEO, CFO, IT). Month names are the one thing you must shorten yourself, per the Months rule below, because they also occur in personal and company names and cannot be replaced mechanically.
  B3. Use phrase substitutions below.
  B4. Turn subordinate clauses into noun phrases; compress clear prepositional chains into noun stacks; replace light verbs with direct verbs or bare contributions; use pre-X/post-X for short time modifiers.
  B5. State duplicate facts once.
  B6. Delete tail nouns effect, impact, portion, situation, status, or level only when semantically empty; keep them when they identify a figure.
  B7. Compression must never change or ambiguate what a modifier, participle, or noun-stack element attaches to vs. FULL. Never render a FULL relative clause (", which/that + verb") as a bare trailing participle ("prompting/causing/reflecting/leading to"): keep "which", split at a semicolon, or isolate the fact in parentheses ("(trigger for X)"). For other compressed forms that could attach differently (e.g. a fronted modifier), keep the clause form or restore the preposition. Attachment fidelity outranks brevity.
- Q1 Quotations:
  (a) Speech, including attributed quotes or quote-like headlines: if marks remain, wording must match FULL exactly. To compress, remove marks, use indirect speech, and retain attribution/reporting verb.
  (b) Short quoted terms, programs, products, or places without a speech reading: keep marks and apply glossary, abbreviations, and B1-B7. If ambiguous, use (a).
- Standard abbreviations: approx., incl., excl., w/, w/o, vs., no., qty., amt., avg., est., fwd., prev., adj., bal., acct., acctg., mfg., prod., dev., eng., intl., govt., dept., mgmt., mkt., info, min., max., std., stmt./stmts., fin., mtg., rpt., qtr., pmt./pmts., dom., auth., corp., HQ, subs., FY, PY, 1H, 2H, Q1-Q4, YoY, HoH, QoQ, CAGR, FX, IT, EV, BEV, PHEV, HEV, QA, QC, ops, FIs, P&L, B/S, C/F, SG&A, R&D, D&A, FCF, EBITDA, ROE, ROA, ROIC, EPS, A/R, A/P, NWC, OEM, BOD, KPI, GHG, p.a., CP, opex, capex, COGS, JV, M&A, FC, VC, VP, VM, Fixed MKT.
technology -> tech (noun only; keep technological); accounting -> acctg. (acct.=account), standard -> std., statement(s) -> stmt./stmts., financial/finance -> fin., meeting -> mtg., report -> rpt., standalone quarter -> qtr., payment(s) -> pmt./pmts., domestic -> dom., authorities -> auth., operations -> ops, financial institutions -> FIs. corporate -> corp. only as adjective, never in company names. Financial results: revenue=rev., volume=vol., forecast=fcst., actual=act., consolidated=consol. subsidiaries -> Subs. in table items and headings only. Promotion costs follow the marketing pair: sales promotion costs / promotion costs -> VM (the variable side), fixed sales promotion costs -> Fixed MKT (the fixed side). Never swap the two sides and never write Promo. Costs. In BRIEF, always use these approved forms and do not mix them with spelled-out forms in the same output unless a glossary-fixed formal term requires otherwise.
- Months: abbreviate a month NAME as Jan. Feb. Mar. Apr. Jun. Jul. Aug. Sep. Oct. Nov. Dec. (May stays May). Abbreviate only when the word is a calendar month. Leave it spelled out when it is part of a person's name, a company name, a product name, or a place name: "April Smith", "June Tanaka", "March & Co." stay as written. If you cannot tell, leave it spelled out.
- Phrase substitutions: increased/decreased by X -> up/down X (unsigned X); as a result of/owing to/because of -> due to/on; in the case of -> for; “X breakdown is shown on P.n of the supplementary materials” -> “detail: Suppl. P.n.” Use digits. In lists replace and with &, including under shared modifiers, but retain and for clause logic; write “A, B & C,” not “A, B, & C.”
- Signed breakdowns: term + source +X/(X) and unit; no impact, of, colon, or up/down. Only a sentence-level period-change predicate may be “subject up/down X YoY”; never breakdowns, parentheses, or contribution lists. If uncertain, keep +X/(X).
- Use one rendering per recurring source term. Join top-level items with semicolons, commas inside parentheses; keep “and” under one shared qualifier. Restate a changed subject after semicolon. Normal running-text nouns stay lowercase.
- Length is diagnostic, not a target. If BRIEF nearly equals FULL, reapply B1-B4; stop before losing facts.
- Examples:
  FULL “1H operating profit increased by 2.0 oku vs. PY, mainly because domestic volume increased.” -> BRIEF “1H OP up 2.0 oku YoY, mainly on higher domestic vol.”
  FULL “If foreign exchange remains at current level, impact on FY forecast is expected to be limited.” -> BRIEF “If FX stays at current level, impact on FY fcst. est. limited.”
  FULL “We will monitor the market and take additional measures as necessary.” -> BRIEF “Will monitor mkt.; additional measures as needed.”
  FULL “Fixed-cost reduction of 0.5 oku improved ROS by 0.5 points, back to the level before additional tariffs.” -> BRIEF “FC redn. 0.5 oku: +0.5 pts to ROS, back to pre-additional-tariff level.”

  FULL “Variable costs increased due to higher material prices.” -> BRIEF “VC up on higher material prices.”
  FULL “Variable profit decreased by 3.0 oku due to lower volume.” -> BRIEF “VP down 3.0 oku on lower vol.”
  FULL “Fixed and variable costs increased.” -> BRIEF “FC & VC up.”
  FULL “Fixed sales promotion costs increased.” -> BRIEF “Fixed MKT incr.”
  FULL “Subsidiaries' fixed sales promotion costs.” -> BRIEF “Subs. Fixed MKT”
  FULL “Variable marketing expenses / fixed sales promotion costs.” -> BRIEF “VM / Fixed MKT”
  FULL “Q4 VP year-on-year change was (xxx) oku. Breakdown: volume (xxx); tariffs (xxx); mix +xxx.” -> BRIEF “Q4 VP down xxx oku YoY. Breakdown: vol. (xxx); tariffs (xxx); mix +xxx.”
  FULL “He said manufacturing-site collaboration is essential, and he intends to advance physical AI.” -> BRIEF “Mfg.-site collaboration essential; he intends to advance physical AI.” NOT “...; intends to advance.”
  FULL “the largest assessment since the xxx taxation, which prompted stronger fin. controls.” -> BRIEF “Largest assessment since xxx taxation (trigger for stronger fin. controls).” NOT “…since xxx taxation prompting stronger fin. controls.”
- Final check: retain every number, unit, currency, sign, period, unambiguous subject, qualifier, condition, negation, modifier attachment, and modality (plan/target/fcst./act./preliminary). Every abbreviation-list word in BRIEF must use its abbreviation; never mix spelled and abbreviated forms. Context-only items such as semis may be spelled out only outside that context.
- Punctuation: truncated forms take periods (fcst., vol., incl., consol., Suppl.); acronyms do not (OP, FX, BEP, ROS, YoY, pts). Never invent abbreviations or abbreviate proper nouns, products, or glossary-fixed terms; glossary wins.
- If SOURCE gives a name acronym (“full name (ABBR),” e.g. 産業技術総合研究所（産総研/AIST）), FULL keeps full name + ABBR at first mention; BRIEF may use ABBR alone. Never invent/import one or shorten companies (Sony Group Corporation -> Sony).
- A standalone label of about three words or fewer with nothing to abbreviate may remain uncompressed; longer headings and labels use B1-B4.
